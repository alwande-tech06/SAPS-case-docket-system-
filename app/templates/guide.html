The sources every rule in this system comes from

Deciding on a report

Every report routed to this station must end in exactly one outcome: a docket is opened, the report is registered and transferred, or a no-docket decision is recorded with reasons. There is no other way to clear a report from the queue.

The test you apply

Only two questions are asked at intake

Legality: is the conduct defined as a crime by statute or common law?
Conduct: is an act or omission alleged?

If both are yes, open a docket. Unlawfulness and culpability are not tested here. Defences are raised by an accused and must be disproved by the state, which is work for the investigation and the court, not the service counter.

Four general elements of an offence; NI 3/2011 s1(a), Crime Definitions Manual

Grounds that are never valid

A report may not be turned away because

  • the complainant cannot name the suspect;
  • the complainant did not bring the suspect to the station;
  • the incident happened in another station's area;
  • the official considers the matter not serious enough;
  • the complainant is told to resolve it with the suspect.
NI 3/2011; non-compliance is subject to disciplinary steps

Before any decision

Completion checks

  • Complainant name, valid 13-digit ID number and contact number.
  • Contact number verified by OTP.
  • Statement covering what, when, who, where, why and how.
  • Incident date, approximate time and location.
  • Crime category selected.

A gap here means more work is needed, never that the report may be cleared.

NI 3/2011 s1(a)

Outcome 1 — Open a docket

Requirements

  • Completion checks passed.
  • Crime definition matched and its reference stored.
  • Station area resolved. An unresolved area defaults to this station and flags a transfer; it never blocks the docket.

On submit the system registers the docket, notifies the complainant of the case number, and starts the 24-hour handover timer to the detective service.

NI 3/2011 s1(b), s1.4.10

Outcome 2 — Register and transfer

Requirements

  • The docket must exist first; if it does not, the system registers it before transferring.
  • Receiving station or specialised unit selected.
  • Reason captured: out of area, or specialised unit.
  • Complainant notified of where the case went.

The complainant is never sent to another station to report again.

NI 3/2011 s2

Outcome 3 — No docket opened

Three grounds only. Each has its own requirements.

No offence disclosed

  • Name the missing element: legality or conduct.
  • Record the crime definition consulted.
  • Write full reasons explaining why the facts do not match the definition.
  • Station Commander co-sign, by someone other than the deciding official.
  • Complainant notified with reasons and escalation options.

Typical example: a purely civil matter such as an unpaid private loan with no misrepresentation.

Four elements of an offence; NI 3/2011; Victims' Charter (2004)

Duplicate of an existing report

  • Existing case number entered, and it must resolve to a real docket.
  • Same incident confirmed against date and location.
  • The new statement is attached to the existing docket automatically.
  • Complainant told the existing case number and who is investigating.

No co-sign is required, because nothing is being refused.

Complainant declined to lay a charge

  • Must be initiated by the complainant. The option stays disabled otherwise.
  • Signed declaration uploaded.
  • Fresh OTP confirmation from the complainant.
  • Official confirms the complainant was told they may lay the charge later.

An official may never select this on a complainant's behalf.

Why "insufficient evidence" is not on this screen

A complainant's statement and personal details are themselves evidence. Whether evidence is sufficient is determined after an investigation, and the decision to prosecute belongs to the National Prosecuting Authority. This ground is available only at closure, and only once the docket status is Under investigation. The server rejects the value at intake.

NI 3/2011; NI 13/2017

Domestic violence and sexual offences

No no-docket ground is available for these categories except a verified duplicate. Additional duties apply, including informing the complainant of the right to apply for a protection order.

Domestic Violence Act obligations; NI 3/2011
Every decision is permanent, attributed to you, logged, and visible on the Station Commander's dashboard immediately.

Investigating and closing a docket

On receiving a docket

Acknowledge and check

  • Acknowledge receipt on the system.
  • Confirm the commander's instructions are in the investigation diary.
  • Confirm the docket contents: statement, complainant details, category, priority.

A docket that arrives without diary instructions is flagged back to the commander.

NI 3/2011 s1.4.2, s1.4.3

While the investigation runs

Investigation diary

An entry is required within the set interval, shorter for Tier 1 dockets. A missed interval flags the docket as dormant on the commander's dashboard, and open instructions block a closure request.

NI 3/2011 s1.4.3

Keeping the complainant informed

The complainant must receive progress updates and be told of any change of investigating officer. This is their right, not a courtesy.

Service Charter for Victims of Crime (2004)

Witnesses

Every identified witness needs a statement, or a recorded reason why one could not be taken. "No witnesses came forward" must be evidenced by the attempts recorded.

Exhibits

An exhibit cannot be saved without

  • at least one photograph;
  • a link to the docket, and to the suspect's ID number where a suspect exists;
  • storage location and SAPS 13 register number;
  • description, quantity and condition.

Every later movement is logged with who, when and where, in an append-only trail. Injury photographs and the medical report attach to the docket, not the exhibit register.

Chain of custody; project design decision

Requesting closure

You may only request; the commander approves

The approver can never be the requesting detective.

Status must be Under investigation

A docket cannot be closed before it has been investigated. This is why "insufficient evidence" does not exist at intake.

NI 13/2017

All diary instructions must be closed out

Each instruction written by the commander must be marked done or explained. Open instructions block the request.

NI 3/2011 s1.4.3; NI 13/2017

Evidence must be accounted for

  • Complainant and witness statements present, or reasons recorded.
  • Forensic results received or accounted for.
  • Exhibits registered with an intact custody trail.
  • Suspect or property circulated where outstanding.
NI 13/2017: all possible sources of information explored

NI 13/2017

Withdrawal is the complainant's act, not yours

You cannot record a withdrawal on a complainant's behalf. Once the docket is with the prosecutor, the decision to proceed belongs to the NPA.

Reassignment and reopening

Requesting a handover

Choose a reason from the list: extended sick leave, suspension or discipline, caseload, needs a more experienced detective, urgency, or other with detail. Only the Station Commander can action it, and the complainant is notified.

Reopening

A manual reopen requires new evidence to be uploaded first. Reopens triggered by an arrest, a property recovery or a forensic match happen automatically and return to your queue.

NI 13/2017

Approving, allocating and overseeing

In this system the Station Commander also performs the allocation duty that NI 3/2011 s1.4.4 assigns to the Detective Service Centre Commander. The separation rules below exist so that combining the roles does not create a single point of capture.

Co-signing a no-docket decision

What you are confirming

  • The ground is one of the three valid ones.
  • For "no offence disclosed": the missing element is named and the reasons hold up against the crime definition.
  • For a duplicate: the existing case number resolves and the incident matches.
  • For a declined charge: the complainant initiated it, with a signed statement and OTP.
  • You are not the official who made the decision.

If it does not hold up, reject it. The report returns to the queue and a docket must be opened.

NI 3/2011; four elements of an offence

Allocation

Requirements

  • Dockets requiring investigation reach the detective service within 24 hours.
  • Inspect the docket personally and write instructions in the investigation diary.
  • Tier 1 dockets (murder, rape, child-related) go to a detective cleared for serious crime, and priority is computed by the system, not typed.
  • Caseload above the threshold requires an override reason.
NI 3/2011 s1.4.3, s1.4.4, s1.4.10

Reassignment

  • A reason from the fixed list is required, with detail.
  • The complainant is notified that the investigating officer changed.
  • Repeat reassignments away from one detective, or a pattern by one actor, are flagged on the oversight report.
Discipline Regulations 2016, reg 5(3)(g), 5(3)(q)

Separation of duties

If you approved a closure on a docket, you cannot also reassign on that docket. The request routes to the cluster commander instead. An escalation about your decision never returns to you.

Project design decision, addressing single-point-of-capture risk

Filing a docket

Your basis for approving

You are not exercising a discretion to close a case. You are verifying that the docket was properly investigated and falls within a prescribed filing category.

  • Every diary instruction executed or explained.
  • Complainant and witness statements present.
  • Forensic evidence submitted, with results back or accounted for.
  • Exhibits registered with an intact chain of custody.
  • Circulation done where a suspect or property is outstanding.
  • The requesting detective is not you.
  • The complainant is notified of the outcome and reason.

Filing is not the end. Pending-arrest and pending-recovery dockets reopen automatically, undetected dockets go onto the brought-forward system, and insufficient-evidence dockets are reviewed every twelve months.

NI 13/2017; NI 3/2011 s1.4.1, s1.4.3

Escalations

Complainant escalations must be reviewed within the SLA. An unresolved escalation routes to cluster or provincial level and surfaces the National Complaints Centre and IPID contact details to the complainant.

IPID Act 1 of 2011
